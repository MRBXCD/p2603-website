AGT: Amodal 4D Reconstruction from Obstructed Driving Videos

Anonymous Authors
Authors and affiliations are withheld for double-blind review
Paper (under review) Code (soon) BibTeX
TL;DR AGT reconstructs a complete 4D Gaussian scene from a driving video whose every frame is partly hidden by the hood, the dashboard, windshield reflections or watermarks. It reconstructs what some frame observes in a single forward pass, and generates only what no frame observes. It is trained on AmodalDrive, a paired dataset built from web-scale real dashcam videos.

Abstract

With the recent advancement of 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) reconstruction, autonomous driving policies are increasingly trained and evaluated in reconstructed simulation worlds. However, these reconstructions rely on clean, curated data from dedicated autonomous driving datasets, which limits their scale. In-the-wild driving videos are abundant on the web, but they carry no annotations and are obstructed by occluders, reflections and watermarks. To bridge this gap, we present Amodal Gaussian Transformer (AGT), a feed-forward model for amodal 4D reconstruction. It reconstructs the full 4D scene from obstructed driving videos, including the parts that camera-attached occluders hide. As the camera moves, other frames observe part of each occluded region, and no input frame observes the rest. AGT therefore reconstructs the observed part from the input frames in a single forward pass and fills only the unobserved part with generative completion. To train and evaluate the model, we build AmodalDrive, a dataset synthesized from dashcam videos and curated driving datasets. Trained only on the Waymo part of AmodalDrive, AGT outperforms prior feed-forward and per-scene optimization methods by 3 dB in PSNR on its test split. It also transfers to other datasets without fine-tuning and to real in-the-wild driving videos. In closed-loop simulation, scenes reconstructed by AGT are more consistent with a clean reference world than those of the baseline method.

Method Overview

Overview of AGT. Given an obstructed driving video, AGT first recovers correct geometry under the obstructed inputs with masked attention and teacher depth distillation. Second, it computes the observability of every pixel from this geometry. Third, it reconstructs appearance only in the observed regions. Finally, the unobserved regions are filled by generative completion and optimized into the Gaussian scene, which is then ready for downstream closed-loop simulation.

Observability across frames

A road point seen directly at frame t is hidden behind the occluder at frame t+1, but it has already been observed, so it still counts as observed. The red region is observed by no input frame.

AmodalDrive

1,178 real occluder layers · every obstructed video paired 1:1 with a clean one

In-the-wild driving videos carry no camera poses, depth or clean targets, so they cannot supervise training directly. AmodalDrive simulates them: real occluder layers are separated from internet dashcam videos by joint photometric reconstruction, their solid parts such as the hood and the dashboard are set fully opaque while windshield reflections stay translucent, and each layer is composited onto clean, fully annotated driving clips. Every obstructed video therefore has a pixel-aligned clean counterpart with cameras, depth and motion labels.

AmodalDrive curation pipeline. The official train/test splits of the source datasets are kept, and no occluder layer appears on both sides.

Observations & Insights

1. Existing methods reconstruct the occluder as part of the scene. STORM and DGGT leave the hood and the watermarks on the road, the scenes of Instant NuRec collapse, and per-scene optimization from four frames is blurry overall. AGT outperforms all of them on every image quality and depth metric, by 3 dB in full-frame PSNR.
2. The heavier the occlusion, the larger the gain. From the lightest to the heaviest occlusion, DGGT loses 3.56 dB while AGT loses only 1.30 dB, so the lead of AGT widens from 2.67 dB to 4.93 dB.
3. A high PSNR in the unobserved region does not mean the scene is recovered. The road is nearly uniform in color, so a blob in a color close to the road already reaches 21.57 dB. The perceptual metrics expose the gap: LPIPS drops from 0.428 to 0.318 once geometry is recovered and the region is completed.
4. The reconstruction transfers, and it matters downstream. Trained only on Waymo, AGT achieves the best results on nuScenes and Argoverse2 without fine-tuning. In closed-loop simulation, its worlds stay closer to the clean reference world than those of DGGT on every measure.
